After: ### How do I calculate work rate as a fraction?
Work equals rate × time. If one worker completes one job in t hours, the worker's rate is 1/t job per hour (a fraction).

To combine workers, add their rates. Example: A does a job in 4 hours (rate = 1/4). B does it in 6 hours (rate = 1/6). Combined rate = 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12 job per hour. Time to finish together = 1 ÷ (combined rate) = 12/5 hours (2.4 hours).

If you are given a percentage rate per hour, convert the percent to a decimal and then to a fraction. For example, 25% per hour = 0.25 = 1/4 job per hour, which you can add to other rates in the same way.

After: ## How to keep an Excel schedule shareable and auditable

Excel will get you a working schedule, but the usual complaints — multiple editors, no history, and no recorded reasons — are solvable with a few lightweight practices that keep the sheet useful longer:

- Use one canonical workbook and a single published sheet. Put the live roster on a sheet named "Published" so everyone knows what to follow.
- Keep a simple change log sheet. Each change entry should record who published it, what changed and a short reason. Paste any Solver experiment into the log before replacing the published grid.
- Record decisions, not just cells. A small "Decisions" table (date, editor, reason) preserves why a shift was assigned and avoids burying rationale in managers' heads.
- Protect formulas and validated columns. Lock formulas and keep drop-downs for shift codes so accidental edits are visible and recoverable.
- Use comments or a short notes column for exceptions (swaps, skill overrides) so the schedule can answer “why” when someone asks.

These steps keep Excel practical for small teams while making schedules easier to audit and hand off.
